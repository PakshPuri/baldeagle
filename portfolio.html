About Me

Hi, I'm Paksh — a student, builder, and tech enthusiast with a deep curiosity for how technology shapes the world, and an even deeper drive to shape it myself. Currently studying at DPS Noida, Sector 30, I've never been content with just learning things; I need to build them.

That restless ambition led me to found CodeKiran, a non-profit dedicated to tech awareness and digital literacy. I believe technology is a language — and every person deserves the chance to speak it fluently. CodeKiran is my way of making that happen, one conversation, workshop, and initiative at a time. Alongside that, I co-founded The Alfaaz Newsletter — a space where ideas meet their voice and words carry purpose. Because building things isn't just about code and tools; it's about stories, communities, and the ideas we choose to amplify.

I'm driven by curiosity, guided by a bold vision, and energised by the belief that students don't just inherit the future — we build it.

Projects

Ares

A 5kg-class RoboSumo combat robot built within 25×25×25cm guidelines, featuring a 6-wheeled Johnson motor drivetrain and a front wedge that slides under opponents and drives them out. Placed 3rd at PRC Jasola.

Velocity V3

A solo-built line follower that placed 3rd at TechRadiance, IIT Delhi — on a first LFR attempt. Built across three versions under strict constraints

Cosmo

A cheerful purple desk robot hand-crafted with a 3D pen. Uses OpenCV face recognition to identify people and waves hello through a hinged arm mechanism — built solo with an Arduino Nano and a laptop camera.

Hermes

A 5kg-class RoboRace bot with an AWD 4-Johnson-motor drivetrain, tuned to 600RPM after extensive testing. Custom-cut motor shafts reduce weight and improve handling. Qualified at PRC Tagore Garden, Top 5 at PRC Jasola.

Epimetheus V1

V1 & V2 V1 is a podium-finishing LFR (3rd, PRC Jasola) with an impeller for downforce, a curved IR array, hand-soldered PCB, and 2000RPM N20 motors. V2 pushes further — the PCB becomes the chassis, powered by dual DRV drivers.

DodgeX

A retro pixel-art dodge game controlled entirely by hand gestures. Built in Python using OpenCV and MediaPipe, players switch lanes in real time by moving their hand left or right — no keyboard needed.

Arduino Radar

A real-time proximity radar using an ultrasonic sensor, visualised through Python. Built without a servo or motor encoder, the sweep was tuned entirely by hand — a first Arduino project that refused to take shortcuts.

PocketRC

A Bluetooth-controlled RC car with variable speed control, operated through a custom Android app. Built solo with an Arduino Nano — a first hardware project that bridged embedded systems and mobile development.

Unity

A static website for a competition built in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript around the theme of unity and diversity. Placed Top 5 — and marks the starting point of my journey that has since grown into full-stack development.

Theseus

An autonomous maze-solving robot using flood fill and shortest path algorithms on an Arduino Nano, with a fully 3D printed chassis. Performed reliably in testing but never got to compete — a capable bot that deserved a stage.

Initiatives

CodeKiran

CodeKiran is only getting started — and it's already changing lives.

Founded on the belief that knowledge shouldn't be a privilege, CodeKiran is a non-profit on a mission to bring tech awareness, digital literacy, and core academic education to those who need it most. Operating both online and offline, it meets people exactly where they are — no barriers, no excuses. In its earliest days alone, it has reached over 50 students and 100 adults — real people, in real communities, walking away with skills and understanding they didn't have before. Beyond technology, CodeKiran has stepped into the gaps the system leaves behind, teaching mathematics and physics to underserved learners who had little else to rely on.

We aren't a regular non-profit, we're an infrastructure. CodeKiran is being built with the conviction that an educated, tech-literate generation doesn't happen by accident — it happens because someone decided to show up and make it happen, in person and online, wherever the need exists. What exists today is just the foundation. What comes next is the point.



Alfaaz

The Alfaaz Newsletter was born out of a simple but urgent observation — an entire generation has stopped reading the news, not because they don't care, but because the news stopped speaking their language.

Alfaaz is here to change that. Built from the ground up for the new generation, it's a free, multi-page newsletter that covers current affairs, culture, and the topics that actually matter to young people today — written in a way that feels like them, not at them. When it launches, it will be distributed directly through affiliated schools, putting informed, engaging, and genuinely interesting content into the hands of students who would never have gone looking for it themselves. No paywalls, no gatekeeping, no dull headlines — just the world, made relevant.

Each edition spans a range of topics across dedicated pages, designed so that every reader finds something that pulls them in. The goal isn't just to inform — it's to rebuild the habit of curiosity, to make staying informed feel less like homework and more like something worth showing up for every day.

Alfaaz is a reclamation — of attention, of awareness, and of a generation that has far more to say about the world than anyone has given them credit for.
